No fault divorce in New York

New York allows a no fault divorce when the relationship has broken down irretrievably for at least six months and one spouse states this under oath. You do not have to prove that anyone did something wrong. Property, debts, support and child related issues still have to be resolved before the divorce is granted.

You must also meet a residency rule. Depending on your facts, this can involve one year or two years of continuous residence in New York, or both spouses living in the state when the case is filed and the grounds arising here. A consultation is a good time to check which rule fits you.

How an uncontested case usually moves

  1. File

    A summons is filed with the county clerk and the case receives an index number.

  2. Serve

    Your spouse is served, or signs an affidavit accepting service.

  3. Agree and submit

    The settlement terms are written and signed, then the judgment papers go to court.

  4. Judgment

    A judge reviews and signs. The marriage legally ends when the judgment is entered with the clerk.

People searching for a quick divorce attorney should know that no lawyer controls the court calendar. Timing depends on the county, the court workload and how soon documents are completed and signed. Reaching full agreement early and returning papers promptly are the parts you can influence. Some cases take months, and others take much longer.

How we approach divorce

Good divorce work starts with listening. Before discussing forms, an attorney needs to understand your family, your finances and what worries you most. One person may need help writing down terms that are already settled. Another may have no clear picture of the household accounts. These cases should not be treated the same way.

We aim to explain each stage before it arrives. You should know what a document does, why the court asks for it and what happens after you sign. When a question has no certain answer, such as how long a judge will take, you deserve to hear that plainly.

Where spouses can cooperate, an amicable path may reduce conflict and keep decisions in their hands. Where they cannot, careful preparation becomes important. In both situations, written records and honest financial disclosure support better decisions.

Your lawyer represents one spouse. Even in a friendly case, one attorney cannot protect two people with separate interests. The other spouse is free to get independent advice, and doing so can make a settlement clearer and harder to misunderstand later.

What you can expect when working together

You can help your case by keeping copies of tax returns, pay records, bank and loan statements, retirement summaries, deeds or leases and any earlier court orders. A simple timeline of key dates is also useful. If you do not have a document, say so. Do not take or access records you have no right to open.

Communication works best when it is clear and timely. Tell your attorney about new court papers, a change of address or a change in your children's routine. Ask questions whenever a term in a draft does not match what you understood. It is far easier to correct an agreement before it is signed than after.

Frequently asked questions

What happens at a Divorce Consultation?

You describe your situation, and the attorney asks about your marriage, residency, children, finances and any court papers already filed. You can then ask about options, steps and fees.

What makes a divorce uncontested in New York?

Both spouses want the divorce and agree on all terms, including property, support and any issues involving children. If any of these stay in dispute, the case is not fully uncontested.

Do I have to prove fault to get divorced?

No. You may use the no fault ground when the relationship has broken down irretrievably for at least six months and one spouse states this under oath.

What if my spouse will not sign the papers?

A signature is not always required for a case to move forward. Proper service matters, and the next steps depend on whether your spouse responds. Ask an attorney to review your facts.

What if I cannot find my spouse?

You usually need to make real efforts to locate your spouse and may need court permission for another method of service. The firm handles cases of this kind.

How long will my divorce take?

There is no fixed timeframe. The county, court backlog, level of agreement and speed of signing all affect it.

Can we meet without visiting the office?

Yes. The firm offers consultations by phone and over Zoom, as well as at 2903 Ocean Ave.

No Fault Divorce

A no fault divorce in New York is based on the irretrievable breakdown of the marriage for at least six months, stated under oath by one spouse. It avoids the need to prove misconduct such as cruelty, abandonment or adultery.

No fault does not mean that everything is automatically agreed. Before the divorce can be granted on this ground, economic matters such as property, debts and support, along with child custody and child support where relevant, must be settled or decided by the court.

Divorce Mediation

Divorce mediation is a voluntary process in which a neutral mediator helps spouses talk through disputes and work toward their own agreement. The mediator does not decide the case and does not act as either person's advocate.

Mediation may suit spouses who can speak openly, share financial information and make decisions without fear. It can be used for property, support or parenting topics. It may not be appropriate where there is violence, intimidation, hidden assets or a serious imbalance of power. Ask our office which mediation related services it can provide.

Divorce Settlement

A divorce settlement records how spouses will resolve the issues arising from their marriage. Clear wording matters because the agreement may guide your finances and family routine for years.

New York uses equitable distribution for marital property. Equitable means fair under the circumstances, and it does not always mean an equal split. Separate property is treated differently, but classifying an asset can require careful review. Debts should also be addressed. Have a lawyer review the draft before you sign.

Contested Divorce Lawyer

A contested divorce lawyer represents you when spouses disagree about the divorce or its terms. Disputes may involve custody, parenting time, support, property, debts or the accuracy of financial information.
